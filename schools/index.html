Ear Drama for schools

Student data addendum. Last updated November 21, 2026.

Students read radio plays aloud together and record them as "ear dramas." Ear Drama is free, has no ads, and is built so a class can do this without student accounts, emails, or stored voices.

For teachers: the one-minute version

  • No student accounts. You type first names (or nicknames, or "Group 3"). Students join with a class code and tap their name.
  • No student emails, birth dates, or photos. We never ask.
  • Voices pass through. A student's recording travels through our server to your device and is not kept on our server.
  • You decide. You cast, review (Approve or Again), and keep or delete the recordings. You can export the class or delete it at any time.
  • Under 13: turn on "Under 13" when you make the class. Students can read together right away. Before their takes can be sent to you, you confirm that your school or district approved Ear Drama, and name who approved it. Under-13 work is never published.
  • 13 and over: you may publish a finished play to the Internet Archive under your name, once every performer agreed and the school has a media release for each performer under 18. Students' names are not published.
  • Every class ends on July 31 unless you change the date, and its student information is deleted then.
  • Check first: if your school has a list of approved apps, ask for Ear Drama to be reviewed. Send your reviewer this page.

What students give us

  • Student name: a first name or nickname typed by the teacher, shown only to the class.
  • Email, phone, address: no.
  • Birth date, age: no. The teacher marks the class "Under 13" or not.
  • Photo, video: no.
  • Voice: passes through only. Sent to the teacher's device and deleted from our server on arrival; never stored on our server; never used to identify anyone (no voiceprints).
  • Location: no. Our server counts requests per internet address for one minute to stop abuse, and does not store the address.
  • Grades, scores, reading levels: no. Ear Drama never rates, ranks, or labels a reader.
  • Teacher's notes: yes, if the teacher types one. Shown only to that student; deleted with the class.
  • Device identifiers, advertising IDs: no.
  • Analytics or tracking: no analytics, no third-party trackers, no ad cookies. The website loads code only from its own address, plus Apple's and Google's sign-in when a teacher chooses it.

How long each is kept: the retention schedule in our privacy policy.

Our commitments

These follow the National Data Privacy Agreement's standard terms. The Student Privacy Pledge was retired in 2025; its commitments are stated here as our own.

  1. School purposes only. We use student information only to provide Ear Drama to the school.
  2. No selling. We never sell or rent student information.
  3. No ads. We never show ads or use student information to target ads.
  4. No profiles. We never build a profile of a student except to run their class.
  5. The school is in charge. Student information is under the school's control. We act as a "school official" with a legitimate educational interest, under the school's direct control, as FERPA allows. We use it only as the school directs.
  6. Minimum data. We collect only what a class needs, listed above.
  7. Delete on request and at the end. A teacher can delete a class at any time. A school can ask us to delete its data and we will within 30 days, and confirm in writing. Classes end on July 31 each year unless the teacher changes the date, and their student information is deleted then. A class no one has used for 12 months is deleted too.
  8. Return of data. The teacher can export a class's information as one file at any time.
  9. Access and correction. Parents and eligible students request access or correction through the school, as FERPA provides. The teacher can rename or remove a student in the app. If a parent writes to us, we refer them to the school and help the school answer within 30 days.
  10. Security. We keep a written security program aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, review it yearly, encrypt data in transit, and store keys only as hashes. Summary.
  11. Breach notice. If student information is accessed without permission, we tell the school within 72 hours of confirming it, with what happened, what data, and what we are doing, so the school can meet its own duties.
  12. No surprise changes. We will not change how we use student information without telling schools first and getting their agreement; a change never applies to data we already hold.
  13. If Ear Drama ends or is sold, student information is deleted, or kept under these same promises, and schools are told first.
  14. No AI training. We never use student recordings or information to train AI.

Who else is involved (subprocessors)

  • Cloudflare, our server and database: seat names, class details, take details; take audio in transit only.
  • GitHub, website hosting: no student data (page requests only).
  • Internet Archive, audio for listening and publishing: only a 13-and-over class play the teacher publishes, under the teacher's name.
  • Apple, Google, sign-in for teachers: nothing about students.

Pictures come from Wikimedia Commons, the Library of Congress, and the Smithsonian; they see ordinary web requests, never student data.

Children under 13 (COPPA)

COPPA lets a school give consent for an app used only for school purposes, in place of each parent, following the FTC's guidance. In Ear Drama:

  • The teacher marks a class "Under 13" when making it; it cannot be changed later.
  • Until the teacher confirms the school's authorization, students can read together, but no student's voice leaves their device.
  • The decision belongs to the school or district, not to a teacher alone (as the FTC's guidance says). The teacher confirms, in the app, "My school or district approved Ear Drama for this class," and names who approved it. If the district has signed our data privacy agreement, that is the school's authorization on record.
  • A school can confirm or withdraw its authorization at any time by writing to us. Withdrawing deletes the class's information.
  • Under-13 work is never published. The teacher can export a recording file to share with families the school's own way.
  • On request, we give the school a description of what we collect and how we use it, so it can tell parents. A parent may ask the school to review or delete their child's information, or to stop its collection.

Agreements we sign

  • SDPC National Data Privacy Agreement. We sign the NDPA (standard version 2) with any school or district that asks.
  • State terms. We sign state addenda (for example, New York's Parents' Bill of Rights and supplemental information, Illinois SOPPA terms) on request.
