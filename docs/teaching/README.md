# The session guides

Nobody has taught this course yet, me included. When I asked what
helps a new teacher run a small online group well, the programs that
have done it for years gave the same answer: a short page for each
session that turns the material into an agenda, and a first session
taught beside someone who has done it before. These pages are the
first half of that answer, and [`COURSE.md`](../../COURSE.md) has the
second.

**One page for each session.**

## What I want

I want someone who has never run a cohort to be able to open this
week's page an hour before the session and run it well, without being
an expert in building apps. A teacher here does not need every answer
about Xcode or Google Play, because the job is to keep the session's
shape, to read what people write, and to notice who needs a word, and
that is what every guide is built around.

| Session | Leads with | What gets brought back | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| The week before | Publish | The app's address, open on a phone | [The week before](before.md) |
| Week 1 | Write | The noticing log, which the session turns into one problem statement, held to the tests and critiqued in the trio | [Week 1](week-1.md) |
| Week 2 | Play | 00 and 01: the problem statement, the two conversations, and the first app in the hands of a person they talked to | [Week 2](week-2.md) |
| Week 3 | Play, then publish | 02 and 03: one verb on both platforms, one cell proved wrong, and the app's own look | [Week 3](week-3.md) |
| Week 4 | Publish, then write | 04 and 05: a screenshot that proved a fix, and the app on someone else's device | [Week 4](week-4.md) |
| Week 5 | Publish | 06 and 07: one honest number and one feature from their own use, shown to everyone; 08 follows the showing | [Week 5](week-5.md) |

Each session opens its week. It is where the week before's work gets
shown, and where the week ahead's work gets started, so every guide
looks back at one pair of stages and forward to the next.

## Before your first cohort

Co-lead one session of someone else's cohort first. Take the parts you
are most comfortable with (the opening and the close are a good start)
and watch how the other teacher handles the groups and the prompt
reading. Then read [`COURSE.md`](../../COURSE.md) once, the stages your
cohort will reach, and [Showing your
work](../path/showing-your-work.md), because the bring-back it describes
is the center of every session.

## The shape of every session

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (curriculum milestone C5, October 7, 2026).*

Every session is one round of Write, Play, Publish, the same three moves
as stage 00, at the size of a week, and each guide names the move its
session leads with and the one question it turns on. The session itself
is a run of show of scenes, in the words the hub uses: a talk, the
rehearsal rooms, a break, a presenter, the design stage, a reflection,
and the question scenes. Prep, week 1, and week 5 change the middle
(meeting the group, finding a problem, and the showing), and the rest
keep this shape. These are the
minutes for 75, and how they change for 60 and 90.

| Scene | Kind | 75 minutes | 60 minutes | 90 minutes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrive | Talk | 0-7 | 0-5 | 0-8 |
| Show what you brought back | Rehearsal rooms | 7-37 | 5-29 | 8-41 |
| Break | Break | 37-40 | none | 41-46 |
| One value at work | Presenter | 40-47 | 29-35 | 46-54 |
| Read one real prompt, or the week's board | Design stage | 47-62 | 35-47 | 54-74 |
| Start | Reflection | 62-70 | 47-55 | 74-84 |
| Check for understanding, and what is still muddy | Question | 70-75 | 55-60 | 84-90 |

The same scenes are in [`runs-of-show.json`](runs-of-show.json), and the
hub makes every new cohort's sessions from that file, so a teacher opens
the class builder to a plan that matches this page and can change any
scene, before the session or during it. Each guide also says what to
watch for, the week's task and the evidence it leaves for the
credential, and which pages of the [stuck library](../stuck/README.md)
to point people to.

When time is short, the design stage gives up minutes first. The
bring-back and the check never do, because the bring-back is the only
time anyone sees the app in someone's hands, and the check is how you
find out what the session actually taught.

## While the groups work

Put the steps of the bring-back on screen in every group's room as well
as the main one, and ask one person in each group to keep time. When you
visit a group, say so as you arrive and tell them to carry on, so
nobody stops to perform for you. You are listening for one thing that
comes up in more than one group, which you name for everyone during
"one value at work."

When a group is stuck, ask which move it is stuck in (writing, playing,
or publishing) and point them to that page of the
[stuck library](../stuck/README.md), which starts every entry with a
question to ask yourself before anyone fixes anything. A stuck moment
the library does not have yet is worth writing down, with the person's
permission, so that it can become the next entry.

## After every session

1. **Read every check before the next session.** Both answers, from
   everyone. Then write two sentences: what you heard, and what you will
   change because of it. Those two sentences open the next session.
2. **Reach out within two days** to anyone who missed the session or
   brought nothing back, privately and kindly. A message like this one
   is enough:

   > I missed you at this week's session, and I hope the week has been kind to you.
   > Your group is still yours, the recording is up, and if you want to
   > show me where your app got to before next week, I would love to see
   > it. And if the next few weeks are not the right time, that is all
   > right too, and you can say so.

3. **Notice the second "not yet."** When someone says "not yet" about
   the same stage for the second time, ask them for fifteen minutes
   before the next session, and look at the app together.
4. **Do the week's own step,** which each guide names.

## What a teacher never does

The course is ungraded on purpose, so a teacher never scores a
bring-back, counts cameras or attendance, ranks one app above another,
or asks an AI to summarize the students for them. Nor does a teacher
tell anyone to use their agent less. The course asks people to keep the
deciding and the judging, and the agent can do everything else.
