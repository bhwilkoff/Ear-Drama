## Our method is the frame

On October 7 I wrote, while this research was coming in: "Make sure any
new loops that we are creating work with the one we have already
established: Write, Play, Publish. We have to make sure that OUR
processes and ideas are at the forefront" (site DECISIONS.md, "Our
method leads the curriculum"). So, this curriculum has one loop.

The three moves are the ones in stage 00
([`00-why-we-build.md`](../../path/00-why-we-build.md)): writing gets it
out of your head, playing makes your thinking tangible, and publishing
releases the work so other people can play with it too. Every round of
every stage makes all three, and the fifteen principles
([`PRINCIPLES.md`](../../human-shaped/PRINCIPLES.md)) decide what gets
written, what gets played with, and what is ready to publish.

The research found other loops and good habits, and every one of them
fits inside a move rather than beside it. This is where each one lives.

| From the research | What it becomes here | Move |
|---|---|---|
| The agents' explore, plan, implement, commit [03, Anthropic's best practices] | How to play with an agent: ask it to look first, read its plan, let it build, check what it did | Play |
| "Give Claude a check it can run" [03] | The agent proves a fix before you play with it yourself | Play |
| The Question Formulation Technique's four rules [05, Right Question Institute] | Five minutes of your own questions, written before the agent is opened | Write |
| Productive failure, trying first [05, Sinha and Kapur 2021] | Your first answer, written before the first prompt | Write |
| Postman's "What is the problem to which this is the solution? Whose problem is it?" [02] | Two questions written about your own hum in week 1 | Write |
| Resnick's creative learning spiral [02] | Already the shape of a round: imagine and create are Write, play is Play, share and reflect are Publish | All three |
| Willison's rule, that you can explain every part [02] | The bar for the final showing | Publish |
| A toy by the end of week one [02, buildspace] | One platform you can see, published to one person the app is for | Publish |
| The Spec's "What have you tried so far?" [05, Educational Model Spec] | The three lines you write before asking anyone for help | Write |

Credit goes where the idea is used, in the lesson itself, and the frame
never changes.

## The arc

My own record shows ten phases from a nagging problem to a running app,
and every one of them is a round of Write, Play, Publish at a different
size [01, "The phases are Write, Play, Publish, at different sizes"]. It
also shows three ways the loop bent: I published to other people late,
play often ran ahead of writing, and the hum itself was never written
down [01, "Where the loop bent"]. The arc below corrects those three
bends on purpose.

Each week is one larger round. It leads with one move, makes all three,
and is built from the chunks in [01], each of which is a smaller round
centered on one question.

| Week | Leads with | The week's question | The chunks inside it | What it publishes, and to whom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | Publish | What do I need in order to build at all? | Set up; meet the trio; find the hum (begun) | The app's address, on the student's own phone, shown to the trio |
| 1 | **Write** | What has hummed in my life for months, and who else hears it? | Find the hum; human-shaped or computer-shaped; the people outside the screen; the why, written first; real data; one platform you can see | The first platform, to one person who has the same hum |
| 2 | **Play** | What is still wrong, and how do I say it so it gets fixed? | Rounds; milestones and decisions; a second platform | Two numbered rounds and a second platform, to the trio |
| 3 | **Play**, then **Write** | How do I know it works, and what does it look like when it comes from its why? | Seeing it work; its own look | The screenshot that proved a fix, and the look beside the template's |
| 4 | **Publish** | What happens when someone I did not build it with holds it? | Someone else's hand; one honest number; the people again | The app on another person's device, and their words |
| 5 | **Write**, then **Publish** | What did I learn that the next builder should not have to, and is this version enough for its people? | A feature from living with it; teaching the agent to remember; enough, for now | The app, the declaration, and what waits, to everyone, guests welcome |

The stages in [`COURSE.md`](../../../COURSE.md) stay where they are. What
changes is that each week now names its move and its question, and that
the first week starts with the hum instead of the setup.

### The three gaps, filled with my own words

The record could not teach three things, because they happened outside
the agent [01, "What the record cannot teach"]. The curriculum supplies
each one from what I have already written.

**Choosing the problem.** "Find the hum" uses my definition from October
6 as written: a human-shaped problem is "the gentle hum of worry or
concern that you have about an area of your life," something "that has
gone unsolved for months or even years," and "most of all, it is
something that you cannot do yourself" (site DECISIONS.md, "Human-shaped
problems"). The student writes three candidates in their own words.
Then they sort, with their trio, a set of problem statements that
includes my four human-shaped examples and my four computer-shaped ones,
before anyone names the difference, and only then read my closing
contrast: human-shaped problems "see the world as interrelated stories
to be told, experienced, and added to," and computer-shaped problems
"see the world as disparate data points to be aggregated, quantified,
and leveraged." Last, they write the computer-shaped version of their own
problem beside it. The sorting comes from [02, curriculum idea 2], and
Morozov's name for the computer-shaped habit, solutionism, is offered as
a "go deeper" reading after they have felt it, not before.

**Talking to the people it is for.** Stage 00 already says why this
matters: human-shaped problems "are rarely yours alone," so "the people
who play with what you publish tell you what to write next." In week 1
each student names two people outside their screen who hear the same
hum, and publishes the first platform to one of them by the end of the
week. In week 4 they go back to those people with the app on their own
device. This is the correction for my first bend: in my record, most
early rounds had one player, and that player was me [01].

**Knowing when it is enough.** My apps kept growing, and nothing in the
record shows me deciding a version was finished for its people [01]. The
last chunk of week 5 asks the question directly, and answers it with two
lines I already use. The fifteenth principle asks for "joy for at least
one person" ([`PRINCIPLES.md`](../../human-shaped/PRINCIPLES.md)), and
the paragraph at the top of `AGENTS.md`, which stage 00 quotes, ends "The
goal is never a slick product. It is a tool that makes someone more
human." A version is enough, for now, when
it does that for the people the student named in week 1, and what waits
is written down rather than built.

## The shape of a lesson

[05] proposes a ten-step page with a question at its center, built from
my Educational Model Spec, the Question Formulation Technique, and the
research on trying first. Restated as the three moves, it fits on one
page and reads the same way every week.

**Write, before the agent.**
1. *The question.* One open question about the student's own app, on a
   line by itself. ("Who is this app for, and what will it never do to
   them?")
2. *Your first answer.* Two or three sentences in the student's note,
   written before any prompt, which is the Spec's "Take 5 minutes to
   brainstorm your own ideas about this. I'll wait." [05, principle 7].
3. *Your questions.* Five minutes, as many questions as you can, none
   answered yet, then mark the one you most want answered this week [05].

**Play, with the agent and the device.**
4. *The work, as conversations.* Numbered prompts the student adapts,
   the way every stage already reads. Each one plays with the agent the
   same way in any tool: ask it to look, read its plan, let it build,
   and have it show you a check [03].
5. *Look on the device.* Open the app where people will use it and write
   down what you saw, because "'it works' is a claim and a phone in
   someone's hand is evidence" (`COURSE.md`).

**Publish, to a person.**
6. *One decision, with the agent closed.* Two sentences: what you
   decided, and what it cost. This is how the code stays the agent's and
   the decision stays yours, which is where the Spec and this course meet
   [05, "Where the Spec and this course pull against each other"].
7. *Ready, or not yet.* The stage's "When you are ready to move on,"
   read by the student against their own app.
8. *Be ready to...* The stage's line, unchanged, which is what comes to
   the live session.

**Around the page.** *Go deeper* offers one extension, never required
and never counted. *If you get stuck* links the library below and names
the stuck moment this lesson most often produces. The page closes with
the four quick lines of the Spec's AI Disclosure Protocol (the agent's
role, your essential work, what you learned, your growth edge) in the
student's note [05].

**The novice path and going deeper.** On the novice path, step 4 starts
with a worked example: one real prompt from my own apps, what the agent
did, and what I checked, quoted from [01]. Going deeper, the student
writes the first prompt themselves and reads mine afterward. Worked
examples help beginners most and can get in the way of people who
already know the material [05, the expertise reversal effect], so the
student chooses, which is the Spec's "The pace and depth of their
exploration" [05, principle 5].

**What changes by tool.** The page is the same for everyone, and each
step gets at most one line per tool where they differ.

| Step | Claude Code [03] | Antigravity [03] | The open path [04] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before the first prompt | Desktop app, Code tab, Manual mode for the first night | The Hub, the Default preset, "Request review" on plans | Ollama, one model, and Aider (or opencode on 32 GB) |
| Look first, plan | Plan mode (`Shift+Tab`) | `/plan`, with comments on steps | Ask for a plan in words; the student keeps it in the note |
| Check | A test, a build, or a screenshot in the Browser pane | `/browser` artifacts | The student is the eyes and describes what they see, since Olmo 3 reads text only |
| Undo | Checkpoints, which are not git | `/rewind`, `/undo`, `/fork` | Git only |
| Remembering | Skills in `.claude/skills/` | Skills in `.agents/skills/` | Sections of a short `AGENTS.md` |

## The live session for each week

Every cohort already gets a run of show built from the six parts in
`COURSE.md`, through `defaultShow` in the site's `assets/show-lib.js`:
Arrive is a talk scene, Show what you brought back is a rehearsal rooms
scene, One value at work is a presenter scene, Read one real prompt is a
talk scene, Start is a reflection, and Check for understanding is a
question. This design keeps that skeleton for every week, adds a break,
moves "Read one real prompt" onto the design stage so everyone writes
their prompt on the board, and gives each week its own prepared
questions, board template, and room prompt. These become the defaults a
new cohort is made with, and every one of them is editable in the class
builder before and during the session.

**The skeleton, 75 minutes.** Arrive (talk, 7). Show what you brought
back (rehearsal rooms, 30: three turns of about eight minutes, each
turn with its own scenes for showing, the one question to understand,
and the three questions). Break (3). One value at work (presenter, 7).
Read one real prompt, trying it first (design stage, 15). Start
(reflection, 8). Check for understanding (question, 5).

| Week | Question scene (prepared, with its kind) | Design stage template | Room prompt | What the teacher watches for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | "What do you hope this app will do for someone?" (short text); "How ready do you feel to build?" (scale) | *The three questions*, practiced on an app that belongs to nobody | "Show your app's address on your phone, and say one thing that hums in your life." | Anyone whose setup is still stuck; anyone who has not met their trio |
| 1 | "Is your problem human-shaped or computer-shaped?" (multiple choice, with a "not sure yet" choice); "What is still muddy?" (short text) | *Hum sort* (new): my eight examples and the students' own, sorted onto two sides | "Show the first platform, and read your why aloud. What will it refuse to do?" | A problem that is really computer-shaped; a why with no people in it |
| 2 | "Which of these is a round, and which is a first draft?" (multi-select, from real commit messages); "What is still muddy?" | *Read one real prompt* | "Show one verb on both platforms, and one cell your eyes proved wrong." | Play that is not being written down; the same complaint sent three times |
| 3 | "How do you know your last fix is real?" (short text); "What is still muddy?" | *Read one real prompt*, with a classmate's real stuck moment, shared with permission | "Show the screenshot that proved a fix, and the look beside the template's." | "It works" with no evidence; a look borrowed from somewhere else |
| 4 | "Rank what the other person noticed first" (ranking); "In one word, how did it feel to hand it over?" (word cloud) | *Write, play, publish*, filled in with each student's week | "Show the app on someone else's device, or tell us what they said." | Anyone who has not published to another person yet |
| 5 | "What waits for the next version?" (short text); "What is still muddy?" | *The three questions*, used on the showing, guests welcome | "Explain every part of your app, even the parts the agent wrote." | Whether each student can name what is enough, for now |

The question kinds are the six the class builder now offers (multiple
choice, multi-select, short text, scale, word cloud, and ranking), and
every answer stays anonymous on the main stage unless the student
chooses otherwise. The *Hum sort* board is the one new template; the
others already exist in the site's `board-lib.js`.

## Tasks that leave evidence

Each week has one performance task: the student does the thing itself,
and what they make is the evidence. [05, part (c)] builds the table
from evidence-centered design and Open Badges 3.0, and the credential
already works this way: the repository is the evidence, a live web app
is the first level, and each further platform where other people can get
the app adds a level (site `assets/credential-lib.js`). In-class
recognitions now go into the signed credential's evidence too (site
DECISIONS.md, "Recognitions in the credential").

| Week | Move | The task | Evidence a person can open | On the credential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prep | Publish | Set up, and open the app's address on your phone; begin your list of hums | The repository; the address | The repository, the credential's first evidence |
| 1 | Write | Choose one hum and write its computer-shaped twin; write the why, the people outside your screen, and one rule; publish the first platform to one of those people | The note's first page; the twin; the live address; two rounds in the history | Level 1, the web; "Wrote down what they value", "Explained a decision their values made" |
| 2 | Play | Run two numbered rounds; read and trim the parity matrix; run a second platform on your own device | The rounds; the matrix with one cell your eyes proved wrong | "Took another round instead of shipping a first draft" |
| 3 | Play, then Write | Have the agent prove a fix on your device; make a look that comes from your why | The screenshot that proved it, and what it would have shown if the fix failed; the look beside the template's | "Tested it the way people will actually use it", "Wrote it in their own voice", "Helped a classmate see their work more clearly" |
| 4 | Publish | Get the app onto a device you did not build it on, through a store, a test track, or a direct share; read one honest number | The install link and what the person did first; the number | A further platform adds a level; "Got it onto someone else's device", "Asked for feedback and used it" |
| 5 | Write, then Publish | Build one feature from your own use; write a skill and a handoff; fill in `HUMAN-SHAPED.md`; decide what is enough, for now | The feature; the skill; the declaration; the list of what waits | "Shared what they learned", "Credited the people and tools behind it", "Found whose work they are building on" |

Every week also keeps three small things, which are evidence of the
thinking rather than the app: the disclosure lines in the note, the
private "Am I Building or Bypassing My Thinking?" check from my Spec's
Thinking Engagement Rubrics (never handed in), and the two questions at
the end of each session [05].

**Two proposals for Ben, not built** [05, "Two changes worth making to
the credential"]:

1. Let the student add `HUMAN-SHAPED.md` as its own Evidence item, with
   the genre "Declaration," when they choose to.
2. Let the student write each level's narrative in their own words, one
   or two sentences on what the app does for the people it is for, which
   is principle 13, the builder's own voice.

## When someone is stuck

My record holds five stuck patterns and a sixth quieter one, each with
what got me out [01, "Where I got stuck"], and [05] proposes a ladder a
student climbs between sessions. Put together and sorted by move, they
become a library at `docs/stuck/`, one page per move. Every entry has
the same three parts: a question to ask yourself first, what to try,
and who to ask.

**Stuck writing.**
- *I cannot find my hum.* Ask yourself: what have I complained about
  more than twice this year? Try: list ten, then cross out every one a
  spreadsheet could solve. Ask: your trio, who often hear the hum in how
  you talk.
- *I cannot say what it refuses to do.* Ask yourself: what would make me
  stop using this app? Try: write that down as the rule. Ask: one person
  from the people outside your screen.
- *I do not know what I want it to do next.* Ask yourself: what did I
  wish for the last time I used it? Try: one round of play on the device,
  then write. Ask: the teacher, since no agent can decide this for you
  [05, the decision kind of stuck].

**Stuck playing.**
- *The agent fixes what I cannot see.* Ask yourself: did I say what I
  saw, where, and on which device? Try: a screenshot, and check for an
  old cached version [01, Tidbits Trivia 79e1fd6].
- *The same bug keeps coming back.* Ask yourself: has the agent tried
  the same thing twice? Try: stop the loop, and ask for a check that
  catches the bug, then a page that remembers it [01, Archive Watch
  ddf6393, 2c0be46; 03, "after two failed corrections, start fresh"].
- *There is not enough real data.* Ask yourself: what is this app
  actually about, in real things? Try: ask the agent to research
  sources before building more [01, Archive Watch 6c5c931].
- *It works, and it still feels wrong.* Ask yourself: can I count what
  feels wrong? Try: measure it and choose fewer, better items [01,
  Tidbits Trivia 718cd2a, 3a3573d].
- *The agent keeps stopping.* Try: a written list of what is next and a
  handoff before every fresh session [01].
- *The tools themselves are stuck* (an account, a build, a device). Try:
  look at the last thing that worked, because the history shows what
  changed; and start in the tool where the work will finish [01, the
  sixth pattern].

**Stuck publishing.**
- *I am not ready to show anyone.* Ask yourself: what would one person
  learn from it as it is? Try: publish to your trio first.
- *I cannot get it onto someone else's device.* Try: the free ways in
  stage 05, starting with the web.
- *I do not know when it is enough.* Ask yourself: does it bring joy to
  at least one person I named in week 1? Try: write down what waits.
- *Life got in the way this week.* Ask: the teacher, privately. The
  stage will still be there.

**The ladder, for any of them** [05]: name the kind of stuck, write three
lines (what you tried, what you expected, what you saw), ask the agent
for a way to understand it rather than the fix, look on the device and
at the last thing that worked, then your trio, then the teacher. About
twenty minutes before the trio is a default the student moves. The
three lines are writing, the device is play, and asking a person is
publishing, so even getting unstuck is a round of the method.

**The library grows.** Each cohort's stuck moments, with permission,
become entries on the lesson they came from, which is the same move a
cohort already makes when it sends one lesson back to the template
(`COURSE.md`, "What a cohort gives back").

## The build, in order

Each milestone ships on its own, is tested before it merges, and keeps
the frame above. Template work goes on `main`; hub work goes on `site`.

1. **C1. Week 1 begins with the hum** (template, 2 days). Stage 00 gains
   "Find your hum," the sorting activity, the computer-shaped twin, and
   the people outside your screen, with my October 6 piece quoted as
   written. `COURSE.md` names each week's move and question.
2. **C2. The lesson page** (template, 2 days). A one-page lesson
   template in `docs/path/`, applied first to stage 01, with its worked
   example from Archive Watch or Tidbits Trivia and a "go deeper."
3. **C3. The stuck library** (template, 2 days). `docs/stuck/` with
   `README.md` (the ladder) and `writing.md`, `playing.md`, and
   `publishing.md`, each entry cited, and every stage's "If you get
   stuck" pointing into it.
4. **C4. Setup for three paths** (template, 2 days). `docs/path/setup.md`
   for Claude Code and Antigravity with the privacy step and Manual for
   the first night, and "If you will not use an AI company" for the open
   path. A short `AGENTS-SHORT.md` for small models, `.aider.conf.yml`
   and `opencode.json` examples, the evidence line in `AGENTS.md`, and a
   test that keeps the short file short [03, 04].
5. **C5. The weekly guides as runs of show** (template, 2 days).
   `docs/teaching/before.md` and `week-1.md` to `week-5.md` rewritten
   around the table above, so a teacher reading the guide and a teacher
   opening the class builder see the same session.
6. **C6. The defaults a cohort is made with** (site, 3 days). Each week's
   run of show, prepared questions, room prompt, and board template
   seeded when a cohort is created; the *Hum sort* board template; the
   class builder offering each week's default. Policy tests for anything
   that touches the database.
7. **C7. The hub says it too** (site, 2 days). `/teach/guide/`, the week
   table on `/cohorts/`, the path pages, and the stuck library linked from
   `/cohort/` and from each scene in the add-on where people stall.
8. **C8. Stages 02 to 08 on the lesson page** (template, 3 days), one
   stage at a time, each with its own worked example from [01].
9. **C9. The screenshots** (my Mac, 1 day). The twenty screens from [03],
   taken with me signed in, placed in the lessons that need them.

**What waits on me.** A read of my own voice in every new lesson,
starting with C1, because the hum is the part of the curriculum most
made of my own words. A yes or no on the two credential proposals.
Whether the open path is tried on this Mac first, which means installing
Ollama and one model. And sitting with the twenty screens in C9, since
they need my accounts signed in.

## What this design does not settle

- **Whether week 1 holds.** It carries six chunks because that is where
  the real work is [01]. If it is too much, "Real data" and "One
  platform" move to week 2 and nothing else changes.
- **How much question time a novice can carry** in a week that also
  holds setup [05].
- **Whether the open path works for a beginner.** Nobody has run it yet
  [04].
- **Whether this order works for someone who has never built anything.**
  The first cohort is how we find out [01].

Write the hum down first.
