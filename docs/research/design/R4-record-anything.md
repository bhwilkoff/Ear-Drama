# R4: Record anything (design for batch B18, 2026-11-24)

A design, not a build. Nothing in the product changed and no binding doc was
edited; the exact amendments are in §7, to apply when the build starts.

**The ask**, owner, 2026-11-24, verbatim: *"All radio and regular play scripts
should be able to be re-recorded. You should be able to record your own version
of anything you want with your own casting."* Decision 088 records it: rights
never keep a script from being **recorded**; **publishing** under CC0 still
needs the script's own evidence. B16 (the Gutenberg parser) and B19 (the Generic
Radio Workshop's text) are separate. This page is 088.3: anything else.

Frames: `docs/research/design/r4-mock/frames/` (28, at 375 and 1440 px, light
and dark, each read). The mock is one file, `r4-mock/mock.html`
(`?s=record|add|parts|line|episode|ear|earline|script|shared&theme=dark&wide=1`),
with the site's token block and fonts inlined; `r4-mock/shoot.mjs` draws the
frames. The words in it are *The Landing of the Pilgrims* (Gutenberg 27357) and
archive.org's automatic transcript of *The Planet Man* 5.

Skills applied: `learning-orientation-design`, `catalog-experience-design`,

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 A defect to fix first: 788 of the 803 plays cannot be shared or read together

The Worker accepts only scripts in `worker/src/castable-manifest.json`. That
file is built from the bundle and holds **15** scripts. The published library
holds **803** (live `data/plays/library/index.json`, 2026-11-24); the 15 are
among them at the same revisions, and the other 788 are unknown to the Worker.
Asked directly, the deployed Worker refuses a library play (`POST /v1/read`
with `gut-3012`, *The Acharnians*, and a deliberately wrong revision, so nothing
could be made):

```
{"error":"not_castable"}  422
```

So **Read together** is offered on all 803 play pages (`js/read-ui.js:63`) and
answers "This play cannot be read together." on 788 of them; **Share** answers
"This play cannot be shared." after a person has recorded; a class can pick from
15 (`js/class-ui.js:290`). Recording alone works for all 803. The clients
already assume a library play is shareable (`PlaysLibrary.swift:111` says so).
It is the first part of "your own version of anything, with your own casting",
so it is batch **R4-0**, before anything new.

### 1.2 What exists to build on

| Piece | State | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A client-side parser | **None** on any stack | only `catalog/tools/script_parse.py` (1,139 lines of Python) |
| Opening or pasting a file | **None** on any platform | no `fileImporter`, file input, or `OpenDocument` anywhere |
| A production keeps its own script | Yes, on every platform | `{id}/script.json` (PLAYS-DESIGN §6; `js/plays-store.js:130`, `apple/Core/Plays/PlayFiles.swift:186`, `android/.../PlayFiles.kt:239`) |
| The recorder, reader, marks, mix, export | Work from that script alone | §6 lists the exceptions |
| Kokoro voices | Need phonemes the catalog built | a line without them is read by the system voice, never left silent (`js/plays-voice.js:155`, `KokoroCore.swift:7`, `KokoroCore.kt:16`) |
| The archive.org uploader | Builds an item even when a script is in no manifest | `worker/src/plays-archive.js:248` and `:465` fall back to `{ title }`: harmless today, a hole the day a script may be outside the manifest |

Research 08b §3 already chose the shape used here: ids assigned once and never
recomputed, a letter for an inserted line, takes attached to the line. 08b §6
risk 8 ("a user who imports Chekhov may bring a protected translation; allow
only imports from sources with rights evidence") is superseded for **recording**
by 088 and kept, whole, for **publishing** (§2.6).

### 1.3 The transcript is a better starting script than we thought

Measured 2026-11-24. Live sample: every 45th episode shard, 1,659 episodes.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| How many episodes show a transcript? | 195 of 1,659 (11.8%); the live manifest counts 8,526 searchable of 73,630 |
| How long is one? | median of 20: 519 lines, 4,420 words, 23 KB |
| Does it name speakers? | No |
| **Does it carry times?** | **Yes.** archive.org serves `{stem}.autogenerated.vtt` beside every `.txt`. 60 of 60 episodes asked had one, and its cues were line for line the text we show (60 of 60) |
| Can a browser read the VTT? | Not at `/download/` (it answers with no `access-control-allow-origin`) nor on the storage node; **yes at `https://archive.org/cors/{item}/{file}`**, 60 of 60. The apps need no CORS |
| Is one line one speaker? | As served: 94.8% of 77,243 judged lines (174 transcripts with a human script to compare); 38 of the 174 were bad (over 10% of lines hold two speakers; the worst about three quarters). **Cut at sentence ends: 99.8%**; the median transcript has 0.17% mixed lines and only 2 of 174 stay over 10% |
| How right are the words? | 93.4% of the transcript's words align with the human script in order (median; the tenth worst 81.5%). The rest is errors, ad-libs, and cuts |
| How much work is casting it? | after sentence cuts, 638 lines and **225 changes of speaker** in a median half hour: one tap about every 8 seconds |

The method is the word alignment `measure_transcripts.py` uses, with each script
word carrying the role `script_parse` gives it, on the cached GRW scripts
(measurement only; none of their text is used here). The sentence cut was
measured without an abbreviation guard; the build adds one ("Mr. Barker" is one
sentence).

In 220 random shards, no episode with a transcript had `clean` rights: the
transcribed episodes are the unclaimed ones. So a cast transcript will
practically never be publishable, and the design says so before anyone starts.

### 1.4 Light parsing is enough, measured

A prototype of the rules in §2.2 (about 70 lines of Python, seven patterns, no
dictionary) was run against the pipeline's own parser on the cached texts. The
number is the share of spoken words given the same part:

| Texts | n | Median | Worst tenth | Words left for the person | Under half right |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radio scripts (GRW) | 140 | **96.1%** | 87.8% | 3.9% | 0 |
| Stage plays (Gutenberg, clean parses) | 34 | **94.5%** | 62.4% | 3.4% | 1 |

(A first version without the "Name." and italic-name rules scored 77.7% on
stage plays; those two rules are why they are in §2.2.) The pipeline's parser
must be right with nobody watching. Here a person is watching, owns the result,
and fixes what is left in a screen built for it. Porting 1,139 lines of layout
families to three languages would buy a few points and three parsers to keep
equal. **Recommendation: a small grammar with goldens on three stacks, and a
good correcting screen.**

## 2. The recommended design

### 2.1 In one paragraph

A person **adds a script** (paste, or open a text file) in Record. The app
proposes who says what; the person corrects it on the script's own page; Done
makes it a script like any other on this device: Record this, Read, your own
cast. Or they open an episode that has no script but has an automatic
transcript, and **cast it by ear**: the episode plays, the line being said is
lit, and they tap who is speaking. Both end in the same thing, an **own script**,
kept on the device. Nothing is asked until they share. Then the text goes to the
Worker, readable only by the people in that production. It never publishes.

### 2.2 Adding a script, step by step

Frames: `record`, `add`, `parts`, `line`, `script`.

1. **Record > Add a script**, beside the heading. (Record is the verb's section,
   075; no new section and no Settings.)
2. **The text.** A title and one field. **Paste** and **Open a file**. The foot
   counts what was found as they type ("9 parts · 297 lines") and says **Kept on
   this device**, a fact they cannot see.
3. **Next** opens **the parts**: the script drawn as the reader draws it, each
   speech under its part's name in its hue. A paragraph the rules could not give
   to anyone is dashed and labeled **No part**, with the two nearest speakers as
   one-tap answers, **Direction**, and **More**. A strip counts them; **Next**
   jumps to the next one.
4. **Fixing.** Tap any line: its words (tap between two words to place the cut),
   the parts, **Split here**, **Join to line above**, **Edit words**, **Leave
   out**. Tap a part's chip: rename it, or **Join to** another part (BARN. and
   BARNARDO become one). **Undo** takes back any step.
5. **Done.** Lines with no part become directions (nothing is lost, and nothing
   blocks). The script's page opens: **Record this**, **Read**, **Read
   together**, its Parts, then the facts ("Added by you · Kept on this device"),
   the refusal ("This script can't be published."), and Edit, Save as text,
   Delete.
6. From there it is the shipped flow: cast sheet, studio, relay, listen through,
   export.

**The grammar (all of it).** Normalize line ends, NFC, drop `_` around a span.
Read row by row; the first rule that matches wins.

| # | A row like | Is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `SOUND:` `SFX` `MUSIC:` at the start | a sound or music line |
| 2 | `ACT`, `SCENE`, `INT.`, `EXT.`, `PROLOGUE`, `CURTAIN`, `FADE IN`, ... | a direction; `sectionLevel` finds it for the contents |
| 3 | `Name: words`, `@Name: words` | a speech, when the name occurs twice or more |
| 4 | `NAME. words` | the same |
| 5 | `NAME` then a tab or three spaces, then words | the same |
| 6 | `NAME` alone (with an optional `(direction)`), words under it | the same (Fountain's character line) |
| 7 | `Name. words` opening a paragraph, five times or more, not a common word ("Yes.", "Enter") | the same |
| 8 | a row wholly in `( )` or `[ ]`, after a blank | a direction |
| 9 | a row under a speech with no blank between | more of that speech |
| 10 | anything else | **No part**, for the person |

A name is at most four words and 30 characters, never NOTE, CAST, SCENE, TIME,
PLACE and the like. Inside a speech, `( )` and `[ ]` are direction pieces, as
the catalog's are. Every piece is a slice of the pasted text: no word is written
or changed except by the person's own Edit words.

### 2.3 What can be opened, natively, with no packages

| | First build | Next (not first) | Refused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Web | `<textarea>`, paste, drop, `<input type=file>` for .txt, .fountain, .md | .docx (`DecompressionStream('deflate-raw')` and `DOMParser`) | PDF (no browser API), RTF |
| iOS, iPadOS | `PasteButton`, `.fileImporter` for plain text; `CFBundleDocumentTypes` so a text file in Files or a share sheet opens here | .rtf (`NSAttributedString`), PDF text (`PDFKit`), .docx (Foundation has no zip reader: about 60 lines over `Compression` and `XMLParser`) | a Share Extension (a second target for what "Open in" and paste already do) |
| macOS | the same, plus drop on the window; needs `ENABLE_USER_SELECTED_FILES = readonly` (the sandbox has no file access today, `project.yml:130`) | .rtf, .docx, .odt (`NSAttributedString` reads them on the Mac) | |
| Android | paste, `OpenDocument` text/*, and `ACTION_SEND`/`ACTION_VIEW` text/plain (the share sheet works for selected text in any app, with no new screen) | .docx (`ZipInputStream` and `XmlPullParser`); PDF text only on API 35 and later | |
| Apple TV, Google TV, other TVs | n/a: they record nothing | | |

Copying from Word, Docs, Pages, or a PDF viewer and pasting covers the rest on
day one. .docx is the one format worth adding next, because teachers keep plays
in it. A file over the cap (§3) is refused: **"That file is too long."** A file
that is not text: **"That is not a text file."**

### 2.4 Casting an episode by ear, step by step

Frames: `episode`, `ear`, `earline`.

1. **The door.** On an episode with no castable script and a shown transcript,
   **Record this** stands where it stands today when there is a script. No
   transcript and no script: no button (88% of episodes; nothing is promised).
   The same verb, so nobody learns a second one. Not on a TV.
2. **The screen.** The transcript in lines, labeled **Automatic transcript** (the
   one label, 058). The episode plays in the app's own player, from the default
   copy, which is the one the transcript was made from; speed starts at 0.75×.
   The line being said is outlined in ink.
3. **Casting.** **+ Part** names a voice the person hears ("DANTRO"). Tap a
   part: the lit line is theirs, **and so is every line after it until the next
   tap**. About two lines in three go on with the same speaker, so one tap per
   change is the whole job, about one every eight seconds. On a keyboard, 1 to 9
   are the parts, Space plays, Left goes back 5 seconds, U undoes.
4. **Leave out** works the same way for a commercial or an opening they do not
   want. A tap on any line opens the line sheet (Hear it, Edit words, Split here,
   Join, Leave out), so an error heard is an error fixed.
5. On wide screens each part has a play button: it plays one of the lines given
   to that part, which is how a person checks whether two voices are one.
6. **Done** any time: what is cast so far stays ("37 of 149 lines cast") and can
   be taken up later from Your scripts. Uncast lines have no part, and the parts
   screen of §2.2 finishes it. Then it is an own script like any other, whose
   source is the episode, linked.

The broadcast's sound never enters the new recording: it is not a cue, not in
the mix, not exported. The lines' times are kept on the device with the script
and used only here.

**From cues to lines** (one rule, golden on three stacks): each VTT cue, cut
after `.`, `?` or `!` (and a closing quote) before a space and a capital, never
after Mr, Mrs, Ms, Dr, St, Col, Capt, Lt, Gen, Sgt, Prof, Rev; a piece's times
are its share of the cue's by characters.

### 2.5 Where it lives (every entry, named)

| Place | Entry | When |
|---|---|---|
| Record, heading | **Add a script** | always (not on a TV) |
| Record, after Yours | **Your scripts**: each title, "9 parts · 297 lines" or "37 of 149 lines cast" | when there is one; absent otherwise (087's rule) |
| An episode's page | **Record this** | a castable script (today's behavior), else a shown transcript |
| Search | a **Your scripts** row when the words match a title, on this device; and **Add a script** under a search that found no play | |
| Class page (teacher) | the teacher's own scripts after the plays in **Pick a play** | signed in |
| Share sheet / Open in | opens Add a script with the text in it | Android, Apple |
| Library | Making, unchanged: productions, as today | a script with no production is Record's |
| Series page, Home, Surprise, Stations, TVs | nothing | it would be noise on pages that cannot answer it |

"The text is mine, or I typed it out" is never asked: it would be an account of
someone's rights that they cannot judge and we would not act on.

### 2.6 What never publishes

1. **The Worker refuses**, before any other check: `publish` on a production
   whose script is not in the manifest with a castable rights bucket answers
   `422 script_not_publishable`, writes no job, and says so in the record
   (`publishable: false`). Clients show **"This script can't be published."**
   where Publish would be, and on the script's page before anyone records.
2. **The uploader refuses again**: the `{ title }` fallback at
   `plays-archive.js:248` and `:465` becomes a refusal, so a job made any other
   way (an old row, a bug) uploads nothing.
3. The same field carries **B19**: a Generic Radio Workshop script would be a
   manifest row with `publishable: false`. One gate, not two.
4. **Exporting a file stays.** It is the person's own act (PLAYS-DESIGN §2.7),
   as a recording made in a kitchen is.
5. **A person's own original work** could be dedicated to CC0 by the person who
   wrote it: they hold the right. It needs the owner's words and is a rights call,
   so it is question 2 and is **not in the first build**; until then the Worker
   refuses every own script. Nothing here depends on the answer.

### 2.7 The learning test

**Should it exist?** Families, classes, and writers have scripts we will never
have, and most episodes have no script at all. **Who does it touch?** The
performers; the writers of the words, living or not (a protected script
performed at home is a home performance; the app helps no further than a tape
recorder does, and §2.6 stops it at the door of publication); the people invited,
who now see text a stranger typed. **What might people stop learning?** If a
model cast the voices, they would stop listening.

| | Bring your own script | Cast by ear |
|---|---|---|
| **Understanding** | Reading a script line by line to say who speaks is close reading; correcting the app's guess is the reading | The deepest listening in the app: telling two voices apart, noticing where one hands to the other, hearing the edits. Honest cost: 7% of the words are wrong, and a person who does not check will perform errors. The label stays on the page, Edit words is one tap, and Hear it is beside it |
| **Participation** | The words, the parts, and the cast are all theirs | Entirely theirs: the app proposes **nothing** about who speaks |
| **Agency** | Leaves as plain text (Save as text), so the work is not locked in | They could do it with a pencil and a tape; the app only keeps the place |
| **Clarity** | Ten rules anyone can read; what they cannot decide is shown as undecided | One gesture: tap who is speaking |

**What would we lose by automating?** Speaker diarization exists. It would be
roughly right, and the person would stop listening at the first screen: the
finished cast would be a machine's, offered as theirs. It is refused as a
feature, not postponed. (It is also voice analysis, which 084 forbids for
students.) What **is** automated is mechanical: keeping the place, carrying a
choice forward, counting.

**The honest judgment of cast by ear:** it is slow. A half hour takes 40
minutes at 0.75× before any fixing, with 225 decisions. Most people will not finish one, so the
unit must be allowed to be small: Done at any time, an uncast rest, and Leave out
make a five-minute scene a whole success. If a first look at real use shows
nobody passing 30 lines, the next step is choosing a scene to cast, not a model.

### 2.8 What it isn't

- **Not a script editor.** No drafting tools, formatting, comments, versions, or
  collaboration on the text. One field, one correcting page.
- **No gallery.** An own script is never listed, searched by others, recommended,
  linked to the public, or given a share page. It travels only inside a
  production or a class.
- **No fetching.** No URL field, no import from a site, no "find the script"
  link. The app does not get protected text for people. (The proxy chain
  `web-content-extraction` describes would send a person's reading to strangers'
  servers besides.) "Read the script" stays a link out, unchanged.
- **No model** writes, completes, punctuates, names, or casts anything.
- **No scanning or photo text** in this batch.

## 3. Identity, storage, and safety

### 3.1 Ids

- **Script id**: `own-` and a lowercase UUID, made on the device. No catalog id
  begins with `own-` (they are `gut-` and `grw-`); a catalog test holds that, and
  the manifest builder refuses one. A server never resolves text by this id, only
  by the production that holds it, so nobody can claim another person's id.
- **Line ids**: `L0001`, `L0002`, ... given once, at the first Done (or the first
  Record this). After that an inserted or split line takes the line above's id and
  a letter (`L0009a`), as the catalog does; a removed id is never used again. The
  script keeps `nextLetter` per line so two edits cannot collide.
- **Revision**: the first 12 hex of the SHA-256 of the lines as canonical JSON
  (sorted keys, no spaces, UTF-8), the catalog's own formula
  (`build_castable_scripts.py:92`), golden on three stacks. The bytes hashed are
  the bytes sent (§3.3), so the Worker checks it without parsing.

### 3.2 On the device

`Scripts/<id>/script.json` beside `Plays/` (Apple Application Support, Android
`filesDir`, web IndexedDB `ed-plays` key `scripts/<id>/script.json`): a castable
script's shape exactly, with `rights: { bucket: "own", evidence: [] }`, `source:
{ name: "Added by you", url: "" }` (a cast transcript: `{ name: "Automatic
transcript", url: <the episode's link> }` and `episode`), plus `times` (device
only) and the pasted original (so a second look is possible). A production copies
it, as every production does.

**Editing after a take exists** (the rule that keeps takes honest):

| Change | With no take on the line | With a take |
|---|---|---|
| Edit words | yes | yes; the take stays, and the line reads **Words changed** in the take list when more than punctuation changed |
| Give to another part, split, join, leave out | yes | refused: **"This line has a take."** |
| Add a line | yes, with a letter | yes |
| Anything, once **shared** | the text is frozen for that production: **Edit a copy** makes a new script | |

The last row is why nobody needs a merge: everyone in a production reads one text.

Deleting a script with productions: "Delete {title}? Its productions keep their
copy." Export my data gains `scripts[]` (id, title, source, created, lines);
**Save as text** writes a Fountain-readable .txt.

### 3.3 On the server (a shared production, a class, a read room)

| | |
|---|---|
| Made | `POST /productions` with `own: true` (title and part names pass the name filter; the manifest check is skipped; `publishable` is false), then `PUT /productions/:pid/script`: the canonical lines, at most **400 KB** and **6,000 lines** (the largest bundled play is 292 KB), by the director only, once. The Worker hashes the body and compares it with the revision; it never parses it |
| Kept | `play_scripts(production_id PK, user_id, body, bytes, episode_id, created_at)`, one row, in D1. `user_id` is who brought it, so the table is in `USER_TABLES` |
| Read | `GET /productions/:pid/script`: members; a class's seats by seat key; an invite's holder by token; a read room's members by member key. Nobody else, ever; no list route exists. The client checks the revision and that the lines match the cast sheet, and refuses otherwise ("This script did not arrive whole.") |
| Limits | 20 own-script productions an account; a global stop at 250 MB (`503 scripts_full`, "Sharing scripts is full."), half of D1's free 500 MB |
| Budget | about 6 rows written a share, 1 row read an open. 500 shares a day is 3% of the 100,000 daily writes all the apps share. One SHA-256 of 400 KB is the only work; **measure it against the 10 ms CPU limit before merging** |
| Goes | with its production or class, on every path 084 lists; with its bringer's **account** (the members' devices keep their copies and takes; a new join answers `410 script_removed`, "This script was removed."); by the owner's operator route; on a take-down |
| Read together | an own script has no anonymous room. It shares first ("Sharing needs an account."), and the room carries that production, so no one can post text to us without an account |

Until it is shared, nothing leaves the device, and 079 stands: no account to add
a script or record the first production.

**Why not pass-through, like takes?** A take may wait until the receiver is
there; a script cannot, because an invited person opens the link when they open
it. Decision 082's "nothing at rest" is about recorded voices.

### 3.4 User-generated text (App Review 1.2) and take-down

- **Filter**: the title and every part name pass `plays-names.js` (they show to
  someone who has only opened an invite). The body is not word-filtered: a deny
  list would refuse Othello. It is seen only by people who accepted an invitation.
- **Report**: a third target, **The script**, in the production's Report (reasons
  as today, plus **Copyright**). The production is hidden from its reporter at
  once and no new invite works until the report closes. **Block** as today.
- **Remove**: `DELETE /v1/operator/scripts/:pid` (085's OIDC route) drops the text
  and holds the production.
- **Contact**: as published. The apps' review notes gain one sentence; the age
  rating's user-generated-content answer and the privacy labels are B23's to
  check.
- **Take-down**: a notice naming an **episode** also removes any cast transcript
  of it (`play_scripts.episode_id`), and the episode's Record this goes with the
  episode. A removed play or text is refused at its next share.
- **P4 is now blocking**: reports reach nobody today (question 3).

### 3.5 Under 13, and classes

A teacher brings the script; it is the class production's, seen by its seats.
Seats have no Add a script in a class and no route to upload text (the Worker
answers `403 teacher_only`). A device whose age answer is under 13 keeps sharing
off as today; adding and recording on that device stays open, as recording does
(nothing leaves it). Class deletion, the end date, the idle rule, and a school's
request delete the text with the class. Class export gains `script` (title,
source, lines). No publish, ever, for an own script, at any age.

### The risks, worst first

1. **We become a holder of other people's text.** Shared own scripts are protected
   works more often than not. Members-only reading, no listing, the caps, removal,
   and the registered agent make it defensible; only reports that reach a person
   make it true (question 3). The on-device half has none of this risk.
2. **Cast by ear is slow**, and most people will stop early (§2.7).
3. **Pasted PDFs and OCR** arrive with broken lines. The grammar leaves them as No
   part rather than guess; a person may meet 200 of them. Paste's count shows it
   at once ("0 parts"), before the work.
4. **archive.org's VTT and `/cors/`** are theirs. The text we show does not depend
   on them.
5. **Three regex engines, one grammar.** The goldens are the guard.

1. **May a shared production's own script (typed, pasted, or cast from a

   with it?** Recommended: yes. Without it a person can record alone and pass the

   the declaration ("I wrote this script...") as a second consent of its own. Not
   in this batch; until you answer, the Worker refuses every own script.
3. **Who reads reports, and how do they reach them (P4)?** Sharing text people
   typed makes it blocking for App Review 1.2. Recommended: you, within a day. An
   hourly workflow (085's route, no stored key) opens one GitHub issue assigned to
   you when a report is waiting, saying only how many (never who, or what), and
   closes it when none is. Sharing own scripts opens only once that runs.
