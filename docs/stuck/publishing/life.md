# Life got in the way this week

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.*

Some weeks there is no time, or there is time and no energy, and the
app sits where you left it. That is not a failure of the method or of
you, and it is the one kind of stuck that is not about the app at all
([research note 05](../../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md#the-ladder-between-sessions)
names it as its own kind, "life," for that reason), and my own commit
history has quiet months in it too.

**Ask yourself first.** What is the smallest thing I could do this week
that would still feel like moving, even if it is only ten minutes?

**Try this.** If there are ten minutes, use them to write rather than
build: ask your agent to tell you where things stand, so the next time
you sit down you start from a clear place.

> I have very little time this week. Read our running note and tell me
> in three lines where the app is and what is next, then write that at
> the top of the note so I can pick it up later.

If there are no minutes, that is fine too. The stage will still be
there next week, and nothing in this course is counted by how much you
did in a week.

**From my apps.** Archive Watch made 17 commits in May and 2,216 in
September (Archive-Watch commit history, read in
[research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#10-living-with-it-and-letting-the-agent-keep-going)).
May was not a wasted month, and it is part of why September could
happen.

**In any tool.** The same in all three.

**Go deeper.** Nothing this week, on purpose.

**Who to ask.** Your teacher, privately, which is exactly what the
course asks a teacher to make room for: a quiet note, no explanation
owed ([`COURSE.md`](../../../COURSE.md)).

The stage will still be there.
