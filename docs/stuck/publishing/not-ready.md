# I am not ready to show anyone

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.*

The app works well enough for you, and the idea of someone else opening
it makes you want to fix one more thing first, and then one more. I know
that feeling from the inside: Archive Watch reached another person's
hands seven weeks after it began, and nothing about the wait made the
first reactions easier to hear ([research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#9-someone-elses-device)).
The research on how people learn says much the same thing from the
other side: trying something before you are ready, and then learning
from what happened, tends to teach more than waiting to be shown
([Sinha and Kapur 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105), cited in
[research note 05](../../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md)).

**Ask yourself first.** What would one person learn from it exactly as
it is today?

**Try this.** Publish small and safe first: to your trio, who are
building their own unfinished things and know what that is like. Ask
your agent to help you frame it so they know what to look at:

> I am going to show my app to two classmates tonight. Help me write
> three sentences: what it is for, the one thing I most want them to
> try, and the one thing I already know is unfinished, so they do not
> spend their time on it.

Then share the app's address and those three sentences, and write down
what they did, not only what they said.

**From my apps.** BOBA Playbook's first beta call turned into six fixes
in one commit (BOBA-Playbook 94ac478, May 20, 2026, after "a call with a
beta tester," prompt archive, same day), read in
[research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#9-someone-elses-device).
None of those six were on my list before the call.

**In any tool.** The same in all three, because the publishing happens
between people.

**Go deeper.** Read [showing your work](../../path/showing-your-work.md)
and pick the form of bring-back that feels least like a performance to
you.

**Who to ask.** Your trio first, then the people your app is for once
your trio has played with it.

Three sentences, two classmates, tonight.
