# I do not know what I want it to do next

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.*

This one feels like a writing problem, and it is usually a playing
problem in disguise, because the next thing an app needs almost always
shows up the last time you used it rather than when you sit down to
plan. My milestone lists grew that way: Bsky Dreams went from "Milestone
5" to "Document milestones 8–27 and architecture decisions from planning
session" on its second day, and every one of those came from using what
was already there (Bsky-Dreams 14d0678, February 21, 2026, read in
[research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#6-writing-the-plan-down-as-milestones)).

**Ask yourself first.** What did I wish for the last time I actually
used it?

**Try this.** If the answer is "I have not used it this week," that is
the answer: put it on your device and use it for its real purpose for
ten minutes, the way the people it is for would, and write down the
first thing you wished it did. Then ask your agent to help you hold it,
not decide it:

> Here is what I wished for while using the app, and here is the rule
> it never breaks. Do not build anything yet. Tell me two small ways to
> do this and one reason not to do it at all.

Choose one in your own words, add it to your running note as the next
milestone, and only then ask the agent to build it.

**From my apps.** Archive Watch's busiest month came after I stopped
planning big and let a written backlog decide the next step: 17 commits
in May and 2,216 in September, once the next thing was always already
written down (Archive-Watch commit history, read in
[research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#10-living-with-it-and-letting-the-agent-keep-going)).

**In any tool.** The same in all three. In Antigravity, "Request review"
on plans lets you read the agent's plan before it builds anything, which
fits this page exactly ([research note 03](../../research/curriculum/03-claude-code-and-antigravity.md)).

**Go deeper.** Ask the agent for a [human-shaped review](../../path/talking-to-your-agent.md#ask-for-a-human-shaped-review)
of the app as it is, and read which principle it says you are thinnest
on.

**Who to ask.** Your teacher, because this is the decision kind of
stuck, and research on how people ask for help says the most useful
help here is a question that makes you more able, not an answer
([research note 05](../../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md#d-when-someone-is-stuck)).

Use it for ten minutes, then write one line.
