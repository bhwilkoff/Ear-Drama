# There is not enough real data

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.*

The app runs, and it is nearly empty, or full of examples nobody would
ever care about. When Archive Watch first ran on the Apple TV simulator,
I typed that there were "astonishingly few" films in it (prompt archive,
Archive Watch, April 19, 2026), and no amount of design work could make
a nearly empty catalog feel like the app I wanted.

**Ask yourself first.** What is this app actually about, in real things
(films, cards, books, places, people's own words), and where do those
things already live?

**Try this.** Ask your agent to research before it builds anything else,
which is the move in
[talking to your agent](../../path/talking-to-your-agent.md#research-first-then-build):

> The app has too little real data to feel like anything. Before you
> build more screens, research where real, openly usable sources of
> these things exist, tell me what each allows, and recommend one we
> can start with today.

Read its answer, choose the source in your own words, and then let it
build the pipeline. You never type data in by hand, because the app
should get its own data the same way it will for everyone who uses it.

**From my apps.** Archive Watch went from that nearly empty simulator to
"Full pipeline run: seed (3k) + full (25k) catalogs from 123k federated
works" in one day, once the agent had researched other sources
(Archive-Watch 6c5c931, April 20, 2026, read in
[research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#3-real-data-first)).
Stage 01 already says real data comes first, and my record says it is
where the first week actually goes.

**In each tool.** All three can research. Claude Code and Antigravity
can search the web themselves. On the open path, a small local model has
no web access of its own, so ask it what kinds of sources to look for,
find them in your browser, and paste the addresses back in.

**Go deeper.** Ask the agent what license each source uses and what
your app owes the people who made the data, which is a principle long
before it is a legal question.

**Who to ask.** Someone outside your screen who cares about this kind of
thing, because they usually know a source no search finds.

Research first, then one source, today.
