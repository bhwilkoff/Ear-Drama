# When you are stuck

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review. Built from my own stuck
moments across four apps ([research note 01](../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#where-i-got-stuck-and-what-got-me-out))
and the support model in
[research note 05](../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md#d-when-someone-is-stuck).*

Somewhere between 8 and 18 percent of everything I have typed into an
agent says some form of "still" or "I don't see," which is 578 prompts
across four apps, so being stuck is not a sign that you are doing this
wrong. It is the normal shape of the work, and this library is here so
that a stuck evening turns into one more round of the method instead of
the end of one.

Being stuck is a round of Write, Play, Publish.

You write down what happened, you play with it on your device and with
your agent until you understand it a little better, and if that is not
enough you publish it to someone (your trio, then your teacher), which
is exactly what the method asks of everything else you make. Nothing
here is a new process, only the same three moves made small.

## How to use a page

Every page in this library has the same parts, in the same order,
because the order matters:

1. **Ask yourself first.** One question, answered in your own words
   before you open the agent. Most of the time the answer is already
   half of the way out, and when it is not, you have the words to ask
   with.
2. **Try this.** Things to say to your agent and things to look at on
   your device. None of them asks you to start a server, edit a file by
   hand, or type data in yourself, because that is not how I build and
   it is not how this course works.
3. **From my apps.** The time it happened to me, with the commit or the
   dated prompt, so that you can see it was real and see what got me
   out.
4. **Go deeper.** One line for anyone who wants to understand the stuck
   moment, not just get past it.
5. **Who to ask.** The person who can help with this kind of stuck,
   which is not always the same person.

Where Claude Code, Antigravity, and the open path behave differently,
the page says so.

## The ladder, for any kind of stuck

When you cannot find your page, or the page did not get you out, climb
these in order. The steps come from [research note 05](../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md#the-ladder-between-sessions),
which draws on the research about asking for help in a way that makes
you more able next time rather than only finishing tonight's task.

1. **Name the kind of stuck.** There are four, and each has its own way
   out: *the tools* (an account, a build, a device), *the loop* (the
   agent keeps trying and nothing changes), *the decision* (you do not
   know what you want the app to do), and *life* (no time or no energy
   this week).
2. **Write three lines in your note.** What you were trying to do, what
   you expected to see, and what you saw instead. Those three lines are
   also the context your agent needs, and beginners leave them out more
   than anything else ([Geng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22614), cited in note 05).
3. **Ask the agent for a way to understand it, not for the fix.** Paste
   the three lines and ask:

   > Here is what I was trying to do, what I expected, and what I saw.
   > Before you change anything, tell me what you think is happening and
   > what we should check first.

   If the agent has already tried the same thing twice and nothing
   changed, stop. Sending the same error back a third time only finishes
   tonight's task, and in Anthropic's own study the people who kept
   their understanding were the ones who asked follow-up and conceptual
   questions ([Shen and Tamkin 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills), cited in note 05).
4. **Look on the device, and look at the last thing that worked.** Ask
   your agent, "What changed since the last version that worked?" The
   history usually knows.
5. **Bring it to your trio.** Post your three lines in the cohort's
   thread or send them to your two partners. A partner asking "what did
   you expect?" often does what the agent could not.
6. **Bring it to your teacher.** Office hours, the room that stays open
   while everyone starts the week, or a private note. A decision or a
   life kind of stuck belongs here first, because no agent can tell you
   what your app should be or whether this is the week.

**About twenty minutes on steps 2 to 4 before step 5 is a default, not
a rule,** and you move it. Struggling with something for a while is
worth a lot, and an evening of the same error is worth very little.

## The library grows

When you get unstuck in a way that is not on these pages, write it
down, and if you are in a cohort, offer it back. With your permission
(and your name only if you want it), it becomes a new entry here, which
is the same move a cohort already makes when it sends one lesson back to
the template ([`COURSE.md`](../../COURSE.md), "What a cohort gives
back").

Find your page, ask yourself the question, and start there.
